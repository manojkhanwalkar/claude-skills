## DMA — Digital Markets Act (Reg. 2022/1925)

**If the project is a gatekeeper's core platform service** (rare), check every Art. 5–7 obligation with counsel. Code-relevant ones:
- **Art. 5(2)**: no combining or cross-using personal data across core platform services or with third-party services, and no signing users into other services to combine data, without GDPR-grade consent; a less-personalised equivalent alternative if refused; don't re-ask more than once a year.
- **Art. 6(3)/(4)**: users can uninstall apps and change defaults; allow third-party apps and app stores (sideloading) subject to proportionate security measures.
- **Art. 6(5)**: no self-preferencing in ranking.
- **Art. 6(7)**: free and effective interoperability with hardware and software features used by the gatekeeper's own services.
- **Art. 6(9)**: end users (and third parties they authorise) get effective, continuous and real-time **portability** of data they provided or generated, free of charge.
- **Art. 6(10)**: business users get continuous, real-time access to data generated by their use and their end users' engagement (personal data only with end-user consent).
- **Art. 7**: interoperability of number-independent messaging services (basic features first).
- **Art. 13**: anti-circumvention — no dark patterns that undermine the above (consent screens, choice screens).

**If the project is a business user or app developer** (the usual case), check that it *uses* its DMA rights correctly and doesn't break GDPR doing so:
- Data received via Art. 6(9)/6(10) portability or access APIs is personal data: lawful basis (usually the user's request/consent), minimisation, retention, security — it's not "free data".
- Alternative payment/app-distribution integrations (e.g. alternative app marketplaces, external purchase links) carry the gatekeeper's security requirements and the PCI scope of your own payment flow (`compliance-check`).
- Don't rebuild a self-preferencing or data-combination pattern your own platform could be designated for (UK DMCC SMS firms have similar conduct requirements).

## DSA — Digital Services Act (Reg. 2022/2065)

Cumulative tiers. Identify the tier from Phase 1.

**All intermediary services**
- Art. 11/12: single points of contact for authorities and users (electronic, published). Art. 13: legal representative if no EU establishment.
- Art. 14: terms and conditions describe content moderation, algorithmic decision-making and human review, in clear language (and child-friendly where minors use it).
- Art. 15: annual transparency reports on moderation (not for micro/small).

**Hosting services** (store content provided by users — comments, uploads, profiles, reviews)
- **Art. 16 notice and action**: an easy electronic mechanism to report illegal content, capturing reason, exact location (URL), notifier name/email (except for some offences) and a good-faith statement; acknowledge receipt; decide diligently; tell the notifier the outcome.
- **Art. 17 statement of reasons**: whenever content is removed, demoted, demonetised, or an account is suspended or restricted, send the user a clear statement: what was restricted, facts relied on, whether automated means were used, legal or terms basis, and redress options. Submit to the Commission's DSA Transparency Database (online platforms).
- **Art. 18**: notify authorities of suspected serious criminal offences threatening life or safety.

**Online platforms** (hosting + dissemination to the public), unless micro/small
- **Art. 20** internal complaint handling for 6 months after a decision, free, electronic, not solely automated. **Art. 21** out-of-court dispute settlement info. **Art. 22** trusted flaggers' notices prioritised. **Art. 23** measures against misuse (suspend repeat offenders and abusive notifiers, after warning).
- **Art. 25 online interface design**: no dark patterns that deceive or manipulate users or impair free choices — e.g. visual prominence of one option, repeated prompts, harder cancellation than sign-up.
- **Art. 26 advertising**: each ad clearly labelled as an ad in real time, with who it's for, who paid, and meaningful information about the main targeting parameters and how to change them; **no ads based on profiling using special-category data**.
- **Art. 27 recommender systems**: the main parameters and why they matter explained in the terms; if several options exist, a function to select and change the option directly from where recommendations appear.
- **Art. 28 minors**: appropriate and proportionate measures for a high level of privacy, safety and security of minors (Commission guidelines July 2025: private-by-default accounts, age assurance proportionate to risk, safer recommenders, no engagement-maximising features targeting minors); **no profiling-based ads to users the platform knows with reasonable certainty are minors**.
- **Marketplaces, Art. 30–32**: trader traceability (KYBC: name, address, contact, ID, payment account, registration number, self-certification) before listing; compliance-by-design interface for traders' required information; inform consumers of illegal products sold to them.
- **VLOPs/VLOSEs (Art. 33–43)**: systemic risk assessments, audits, a non-profiling recommender option, ad repository, data access for researchers, crisis response — counsel-led; flag if user counts approach 45 m.

Code checks: report-content endpoint and form fields (Art. 16), moderation actions emit a statement-of-reasons message and record (Art. 17), an appeal flow (Art. 20), ad rendering includes a label and "why am I seeing this" (Art. 26), recommender settings (Art. 27), age signals reach ad and recommender code (Art. 28).

## AI — AI Act (Reg. 2024/1689 as amended by Reg. 2026/1744)

Classify every AI feature (model call, scoring model, recommender, agent) by risk and by your role (provider = builds or puts its name on it; deployer = uses it professionally).

**Prohibited practices (Art. 5, since 2 Feb 2025)** — Critical if present: subliminal or purposefully manipulative techniques causing significant harm; exploiting vulnerabilities due to age, disability or socio-economic situation; social scoring leading to unjustified detrimental treatment; predicting criminal offences solely from profiling; untargeted scraping of facial images; emotion recognition in workplaces and education (except medical/safety); biometric categorisation inferring sensitive traits; real-time remote biometric identification in public for law enforcement (narrow exceptions). Two further prohibitions added by the 2026 omnibus apply from 2 Dec 2026 — check the consolidated text.

**AI literacy (Art. 4, since 2 Feb 2025)**: staff who operate AI systems have sufficient AI literacy — organisational; list under "not checked".

**Transparency (Art. 50, since 2 Aug 2026)**
- **50(1)** providers: people are informed they are interacting with an AI system (chatbots, voice agents, AI agents that email/call people) unless obvious from context. Check the chat UI, email footers, voice greeting.
- **50(2)** providers of generative AI: synthetic audio, image, video and text outputs are **marked in a machine-readable format and detectable** as AI-generated (watermarks, C2PA metadata) — grace to 2 Dec 2026 for systems already on the market.
- **50(3)** deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorisation inform the people exposed.
- **50(4)** deployers disclose deep fakes, and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest (unless human-reviewed with editorial responsibility).
- Information given clearly at the latest at first interaction, and accessibly.

**High-risk (Annex III; obligations from 2 Dec 2027)** — includes **evaluating creditworthiness or establishing the credit score of natural persons** (except fraud detection), life and health insurance pricing, access to essential public and private services, employment and worker management, education, biometrics, critical infrastructure, law enforcement, migration, justice. If a feature is high-risk, start the design now:
- Providers: risk management system (Art. 9); data governance — relevant, representative, error-checked training data, bias examination (Art. 10); technical documentation (Art. 11, Annex IV); **automatic logging of events** over the lifetime for traceability (Art. 12); instructions for use and transparency to deployers (Art. 13); **human oversight** designed in — the ability to understand, monitor, override, stop (Art. 14); accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity incl. against data poisoning, adversarial inputs, model flaws (Art. 15); quality management system (Art. 17); conformity assessment, CE marking, EU database registration (Art. 43, 49); post-market monitoring and serious-incident reporting (Art. 72, 73).
- Deployers (Art. 26–27): use per instructions, assign competent human oversight, monitor and keep logs ≥ 6 months, inform workers; **fundamental rights impact assessment** for credit scoring and insurance deployers and public bodies; **Art. 86 right to explanation** of individual decisions — clear and meaningful explanation of the AI's role and the main elements of the decision.
- Code checks now: decision logs with inputs, model version and output; an override/human-review path; explanation generation; bias tests on protected characteristics or proxies; robustness tests; model and dataset versioning.

**General-purpose AI models (since 2 Aug 2025)**: only if the project *trains or releases* a GPAI model — technical documentation, copyright policy, training-content summary; systemic-risk duties above 10^25 FLOPs. Using an API model is not this (you're a downstream provider/deployer).

Overlaps: GDPR Art. 22 automated decisions, `owasp-llm-top10` for security of LLM features.

## DATA — Data Act (Reg. 2023/2854)

- **Connected products and related services (Art. 3–5)**: product data and related-service data accessible to the user by design (from 12 Sep 2026 for new products) — directly or on request, easily, securely, free, in a structured machine-readable format, continuously and in real time where feasible; sharing with third parties at the user's request; pre-contract information about what data is generated.
- **Data holders (Art. 8–12)**: fair, reasonable, non-discriminatory terms when sharing with data recipients; trade-secret protections; data holders may not use non-personal product data to derive insights into the user's economic situation that could undermine the user's commercial position, and may use product data only as agreed with the user (Art. 4).
- **Users and third parties (Art. 4(10), 6(2)(e))**: data obtained through access/sharing rights must not be used to develop a product that competes with the connected product it came from.
- **Cloud/SaaS switching (Art. 23–31)**: customers can switch provider or go on-premises — export of exportable data and digital assets in open, interoperable formats; max 2 months' notice, 30-day transition; egress/switching charges abolished from 12 Jan 2027. Code check: complete export API/tooling, documented formats, no lock-in by design.
- **Unfair contractual terms in B2B data contracts (Art. 13)** and **safeguards against unlawful government access to non-personal data (Art. 32)** — contractual/organisational.

## EAA — European Accessibility Act (Dir. 2019/882)

For in-scope consumer services (e-commerce, consumer banking and payments, e-books, communications, transport booking) — websites, mobile apps, e-tickets, customer support:
- **WCAG conformance** (EN 301 549 → WCAG 2.1 AA for web and app content; aim for 2.2 AA) is checked and fixed by `ada-compatibility`. Cite its findings as `EAA / EN 301 549 <clause>`; don't re-check the success criteria here. If it hasn't run on this change, recommend it and note "accessibility: not checked".
- What the EAA adds on top:
  - Banking extras: identification, electronic signatures, security and payment methods perceivable, operable, understandable and robust; information understandable at a level not exceeding B2 (CEFR).
  - An **accessibility statement** (service conditions describing how the service meets the requirements), kept current.
  - Support services (help desk, call centre) give information on the service's accessibility features and compatibility with assistive technology.
  - EN 301 549 clauses beyond WCAG for non-web software and documentation (Clauses 11 and 12) where a native app or PDF documentation ships.

## CRA — Cyber Resilience Act (Reg. 2024/2847)

Applies to products with digital elements (including software sold or licensed as a product, apps, firmware, libraries placed on the market commercially):
- **Reporting (from 11 Sep 2026)**: actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents → early warning within **24 h**, notification within **72 h**, final report (14 days after a fix for vulnerabilities; 1 month for incidents) via ENISA's Single Reporting Platform; inform users. Code-side readiness: a security contact and vulnerability-intake channel (`SECURITY.md`, `security.txt`), a way to know which versions are deployed where, and alerting.
- **Essential requirements (from 11 Dec 2027)**: secure by default configuration; no known exploitable vulnerabilities at release; protection of confidentiality and integrity (encryption, authentication, access control); data minimisation; availability and resilience (DoS); minimal attack surface; logging and monitoring of relevant internal activity with user opt-out; secure deletion of data and settings; **security updates** for the support period (≥ 5 years typically) — separate from feature updates where possible, automatic by default with opt-out; an **SBOM** of top-level dependencies; a coordinated vulnerability disclosure policy; technical documentation and conformity assessment (self-assessment for default class; third-party for important/critical classes).
- Open-source stewards: lighter regime. Pure SaaS: generally out of scope (NIS2 instead).

## NIS2 (Dir. 2022/2555)

For essential/important entities: Art. 21 risk-management measures — incident handling, business continuity and backups, supply-chain security, secure development and vulnerability handling and disclosure, policies on cryptography and encryption, access control and asset management, MFA, secure communications; Art. 23 reporting (24 h early warning, 72 h notification, 1-month final). Code checks overlap with `security-review`, `resiliency` and `compliance-check` — cite NIS2 where the entity is in scope.

## DORA (Reg. 2022/2554)

For EU financial entities: ICT risk-management framework (Art. 5–16) — asset inventory, protection and prevention (encryption, access, patching), detection, response and recovery (backups with restore tests, RTO/RPO), learning; ICT incident classification and reporting (major incidents: initial notification within 4 h of classification and 24 h of awareness); digital operational resilience testing (TLPT for significant entities); **ICT third-party risk** — register of information on all ICT contracts, exit strategies, contractual clauses (Art. 28–30). Code-side: resilience and recovery evidence, third-party inventory from dependencies and outbound calls, exit/portability.
